# AKAB Portal — notes for Claude Code

Portal for AKAB Informatique (MSP, Lanaudière): internal team first, clients next.
Brand follows akab.ca: ink `#0a0a0b`, paper `#f4f4f1`, gold gradient
`#ffe24a → #ffce1a → #f59410`, Bricolage Grotesque (display) + Instrument Sans
(body). Logo: `src/assets/akab-logo.webp` through `src/components/brand/Logo.tsx`
(`Logo` full, `LogoMark` bars + cursor, `Wordmark` lettering). The word AKAB as a
brand mark is always the `Wordmark` image, never typed in a font. Dark theme by default.
Any UI or design work (new page, section, component, restyle): load the
`design-taste-frontend` skill first and follow it. French by default, every text in `src/i18n/fr.ts`
**and** `src/i18n/en.ts`.

## Branches

- `master` is production. Work on `feat/...` / `fix/...` branches, merge when `npm run build` and `npm test` pass.
- `master` is the only branch on GitHub; no other branches or tags are kept.

## Commands

- `npm run dev` — web :5173 + API :3001 (PGlite in `.data/`, delete it to reset)
- `npm test` — API tests (vitest, in-memory PGlite). Add a test for every new route/permission.
- `npm run build` — type-check (web + server) and bundle. Must pass before committing.
- `npm run db:generate` — after changing `server/db/schema.ts`, generates a migration in `drizzle/` (commit it). Migrations run at startup.

## Adding a module

1. `shared/permissions.ts`: add the module and its rights (labels FR/EN). The Administrator role gets every staff right automatically.
2. API: new file in `server/routes/`, mount it in `server/app.ts`, guard every route with `requirePermission("<key>")` (and `requireStaff` for staff-only areas).
3. UI: page in `src/pages/`, entry in `src/modules/registry.ts` (remove `upcoming`), route in `src/App.tsx`.
4. Tests in `tests/`.

## Weekly meeting (Rencontre hebdomadaire)

Administration tab `/meetings`, used live during the Monday team meeting (lists refresh every 10–30 s).

- Rights: `meetings.view` (see the page) and `meetings.manage` (change anything on it). Administrators only by default.
- API: `server/routes/meetings.ts`, mounted at `/api/meetings`. UI: `src/pages/meetings/` (`MeetingsPage`, `Points`, `Projects`, `Absences`).
- Sections, top to bottom:
  - **Points à discuter** (`meeting_points`, half width): a fast text field; checked off when settled; settled ones stay visible 7 days. A click opens the point's notes (`meeting_point_notes`, oldest first, `/points/:id/notes`). A point can be put off (`snooze_until`, YYYY-MM-DD Québec, after today: off the list until that day; `GET /points/later`, "Reportés (n)" to bring one back) or archived (`archived_at`; `GET /points/archive`, the "Archives" toggle of the same card, with a search; restore = back on the list). Migration `0045`. Beside it, **La semaine** (`GET /week?from=YYYY-MM-DD`, `Week.tsx`): Monday–Sunday; a day shows the team planning blocks (as `asSeen`/`seesFull` allow), absences, open tickets due and projects due that day, and Québec holidays. Events (`meeting_events`, migrations 0041–0042: date, optional HH:MM start/end, title, note, optional person, optional ticket — only its id is taken from the browser, number/title read from the ticket source) and absences are added/edited from the selected day (`/events` POST/PUT/DELETE, `meetings.manage`).
  - **Billets en retard et à échéance cette semaine** (`GET /overdue`): open tickets from the ticket source (the portal's database, or sample data locally) past due, or due by the end of Friday in Québec time (`fridayEnd` / `fridayDate`; on a weekend, the coming Friday). Shown as two gauges (overdue / due by Friday), green → red at 25, critical at 35; under each, every technician (and the unassigned tickets) as a chip in the same colors — picking one shows their count on the gauge. A third gauge (`stray` in `/overdue`): open tickets with no technician whose status is past "new" (any due date), red at 5, critical at 10, chips per status. Clicking a gauge opens a short list of its tickets (for the person picked, if any); clicking the list opens Tickets with the same filter: overdue `view=due&due=overdue`, this week `view=all&statusKind=<open kinds>&due=week` (portal filter: not overdue, due by `fridayEnd`), stray `view=unassigned&statusKind=<open kinds but new>`, plus `assignee=<id|none>`. The three gauges count only some ticket queues: by default every queue whose name does not contain "alert" (monitoring alerts), or the ones a `meetings.manage` user picked (`PUT /gauge-queues`, stored in `integrations` provider `meetings`, `gaugeQueues`; `null` = default); their links add `queue=`. Monitoring alerts (`isAlert` in `server/routes/tickets.ts`: alert queue, or a source containing alert/monitor) are left out of those gauges, their links (`alerts=0`) and La semaine; a fourth gauge lists the open alerts (newest first, red at 10, critical at 20): a click opens the ticket over the meeting (ticket workspace `ws.open`, closing it comes back to the refreshed meeting; La semaine ticket links do the same), "Résolu" sets `completeStatus` through `PATCH /api/tickets/:id` (`tickets.edit`); Tickets filter `alerts=1` shows only them.
  - **Projets en cours** (`meeting_projects`, `meeting_project_members`, `meeting_project_notes`): client (free text), due date (YYYY-MM-DD), team members (active staff only), notes newest first. Done projects are hidden behind "Terminés".
  - Absences (`meeting_absences`): one row per person and date range (both days included), kind `vacation | sick | training | personal | other`. No month calendar any more: they show and are added in **La semaine** at the top (`AbsenceDialog` in `Absences.tsx`).
  - **Tendances des billets** (under the projects, `Trends.tsx`, `GET /trends?weeks=4..52`): week by week (Monday, Québec) created / resolved / net / open at the end of the week / average and median resolution time / resolved within 24 h, from `TicketSource.history(since)` (light rows: created and completed dates; sample data seeded by the day), same queues as the gauges, no monitoring alerts. Open at a past moment = open now − came in since and still open + already there and resolved since. The last 7 days are compared with the same 7 days 1…N weeks back (slider, quick chips, or a click on a week in the chart or table).
  - **Projets en cours** will be linked to the sidebar **Projets** section (upcoming module `projects`, own sidebar section) once it is built.
- **The week ahead** (top of the page, `src/pages/meetings/WeekBrief.tsx`; `GET /api/meetings/brief?from=` (`meetings.view`), `POST /brief` (`meetings.manage`, 10 an hour) regenerates; `server/lib/weekBrief.ts`, table `meeting_week_briefs` (one per Monday), migration `0082`, AI feature `meetings.week`): 5–8 short bullets on what the week looks like and 3–5 management suggestions. `gatherFacts` reads the same data as this page (absences, team events, projects due and late, tickets due / late / unassigned by the gauges' queues, alerts) plus each technician's week as My day works it out (`dayItems` up to Friday — what is due or late and pinned —, capacity from the work hours less absences and Outlook events); the AI writes from a compact digest (`digest`), the portal's rules (`rulesBrief`: overloaded and free people, unassigned tickets, absences, late work) write it when the AI is off or fails. The week is this one Monday–Friday, the next one over a weekend (`briefWeek`). The portal's own brief is rebuilt on each read, an AI one is kept until regenerated; **each Monday** the morning run (`pipelineTick`) also writes it with the AI.
- Absences are shared with the Tickets planning calendar: `GET /api/planning/absences` (same access rules as `/planning/calendar`) hatches absent days in `WeekCalendar`, and creating or moving a block on an absent day returns the `absent` warning (still saved).
- Migrations `0023`–`0026`. Tests: `tests/api.test.ts` ("weekly meeting: …") and `tests/meetings.test.ts` (Friday cut-off).

## Pause café (coffee break)

Team › Pause café (`/breakroom`, right `breakroom.use`, technicians by default; API `server/routes/breakroom.ts`, UI `src/pages/breakroom/BreakroomPage.tsx`).
- **30 minutes a day** (Québec day): the page posts `/beat` every 30 s while it is visible; the time since the previous beat counts when it is a minute or less (`breakroom_usage`, migration 0053). Once used, every route but `/status` and `/beat` answers 403 `break_over` and the page shows "La pause est terminée" until tomorrow.
- **Échecs** (chess, `chess.js` on both sides): colleagues online = staff with a session active in the last 3 min (`GET /online`). Invite (`POST /chess`, notification `breakroom.invite` → `/breakroom?game=<id>`; one open game per pair), the invited one accepts or declines (`/respond`); the one who invited plays white. Every move is checked on the server (`/move` `{ from, to, promotion? }`; the game is replayed from its PGN so repetition counts; a concurrent change answers `game_changed`); ends on checkmate, stalemate, draw or `/resign` (which also cancels an unanswered invitation). `chess_games` (FEN + PGN, migration 0053; the earlier checkers table is dropped in 0052). The board polls every 1.5 s. Tests: `tests/api.test.ts` ("coffee break").
- **Complots** (Rikki Tahta's bluffing game, Ferti; French Coup): `server/lib/complots.ts` is the rules engine (pure functions on a JSON state: `newGame`, `play`, `tick` for timeouts — answer 15 s, turn 90 s, choices 30–45 s —, `leave`, `viewFor` hides other players' cards); tests `tests/complots.test.ts`. Tables `complots_games` (migration 0056: host, `seats` with answers, the 5 `chars`, `state`, `rev` for concurrent writes; one change at a time per table in-process). Routes `/api/breakroom/complots` (list, create + `breakroom.complots` notification, `/respond`, `/start` by the host with 2–6 who said yes, `/play`, `/leave`). UI `src/pages/breakroom/Complots.tsx` (custom cards in the clans' colours). One character per clan: Complots' Duchesse, Assassin, Comtesse, Capitaine, Ambassadeur / Inquisiteur; from Complots 2 only the Bourreau and the Justicier (their powers are in its rules; the other 7 cards' texts are not).
- **Orbi l'extraterrestre**: the channel's public feed (`GET /videos`: YouTube RSS of channel `UCWwdio93JMiS987S0lo6RXg`, read by the server, kept 30 min, last list kept on failure), played in a popup through `youtube-nocookie.com` (CSP `frame-src`; thumbnails from `i.ytimg.com` in `img-src`).

## AI assistant of the tickets page

One AI icon on the Tickets page replaces the "Plan my tickets" button (`Assistant.tsx`, `POST /api/tickets/ai/assistant`, `tickets.ai`, feature `tickets.assistant`, 30 an hour, audited `ticket.ai_assistant`). A request in words; the AI is shown **only the tickets the person may see** (their own open ones and the ones whose number they name) and told **only the actions their rights allow**: change due day / checkpoint / start / priority / status / assignee, add a note, plan my tickets (opens the usual planning window), and **delete only with `tickets.delete`** (`tickets.edit` for the rest). It has no access to billing, accounting, HR or other modules. **The AI does nothing itself**: what it proposes is checked again (action allowed, ticket among those shown, status / priority / technician values real) and comes back as a list to **tick and apply**; each action is then sent through the normal routes, which check the rights once more (a deletion also asks a confirmation). **It is a conversation**: the dialog keeps the whole discussion and sends the earlier turns (`history`, 16 at most, with the proposals and whether they were applied) with each message, so when the AI needs a precision (which ticket? which date?) it asks, you answer, and it continues; tickets named earlier in the talk stay in view. Ticket text is given as data, never instructions. Actions also include `sort_list` (changes the list sort on screen, no right needed, applied by the page itself — `onSort`) and `set_estimate`; the AI also sees the open unassigned tickets and **the tickets on the screen** (`visible` ids sent by the page, checked against the ticket list — any technician's), and the checkpoint time is read in free form ("9h30"); `update_ticket` = several changes of one ticket in one action (a list of 22 tickets stays short; `then` = the extra calls the page sends); ids may be ticket numbers, dates may carry a time; what cannot be read is counted (`dropped`, shown in orange) and, when the AI described changes but listed none, the server asks it once more for the list. ## ITCloud (vendor; Admin › Intégrations › ITCloud)

`server/lib/itcloud.ts`, routes `/api/admin/itcloud` (`server/routes/itcloud.ts`, right `admin.integrations`), UI `src/admin/ITCloudIntegration.tsx`, migration `0094`, tick every 15 min from `server/index.ts` (`itcloudTick`, only when on and "autoSync"). Keys: integration key in `integrations.config`, private key PEM **sealed** in `integrations.secret`, never sent back (`hasKey`); only GET calls (customers, products, services-report; the report is limited to 30 a minute), **nothing is ever written to ITCloud**. Switching it on makes the vendor **ITCloud** in Admin › PSA › Fournisseurs (`ensureVendor`).
- **1. Customers ↔ clients** (`itcloud_clients`: a customer has one client, a client may have several customers; `GET|PUT /clients`): proposed by the same name (accents, case, punctuation, inc/ltée… ignored) or the same email (client, billing email or a contact), only when unambiguous; applied only once accepted (one by one, or "accept all"), removable.
- **2. Products → services** (`GET|POST /services/plan`): each **recurring, not usage-based** ITCloud product becomes a portal service (Inventory › Services) with `sku = ITC-<productId>`, `vendor_id` = ITCloud, `vendor_sku` = the product id (`services` columns `sku`, `vendor_id`, `vendor_sku`, unique); monthly or yearly from its cycles; **a new service is made with no price and no cost (0): they are typed by hand in Inventory › Services and ITCloud never sets or changes them** on their own. **On demand only**, the button **"Synchroniser les prix depuis ITCloud"** (`POST /api/admin/itcloud/services/prices { applyToContracts }`, `resyncServicePrices`, audited `itcloud.prices_resynced`) puts on every ITCloud service the **cost = ITCloud's unit price** (the product's `monthlyUnitPrice` / `annuallyUnitPrice`) and the **price = ITCloud's list price** (MSRP from the invoice items report, `msrpByProduct`; a product with no list price keeps its price, nothing is invented), asks whether the ITCloud lines of running contracts follow or only the services (future additions), and says the **margin it comes out to** (average, how many are under 15 %; the review list also marks each row's margin, red at 0 or less). Reviewed first (new / update / same), applied for the ticked ones; a service with the same name and no vendor is tied instead of duplicated.

Sync** (`POST /sync`, `syncItcloud`): the services report is kept in `itcloud_services` (with each row's state: `synced`, `client_unlinked`, `product_unmatched`, `no_contract`, `inactive`); every **Active** service of a matched customer whose product (matched by group + name to its product id, else by name when unique) is a portal service becomes a line of the client's contract (`contract_services` with `quantity`, `source = itcloud`, `external_id` = ITCloud service id; unit price and unit cost = **the portal service's, typed by hand, copied when the line is made** (the sync never overwrites them afterwards; they change only through the questions of "Service prices" below), start day = **the contract's start day** (never ITCloud's registration date; an existing line is moved to it at the next sync); a service added by hand also starts on the contract's start day unless another day is picked). **There is no automatic contract**: a service waits **unmapped** (state `no_contract`, "À associer à un contrat") until it is mapped to one of the client's active contracts — by **customer** in step 1 (a select on each matched row, `itcloud_clients.contract_id`, migration `0095`, `PUT /clients/:id/contract`: all that customer's services land on it at the next sync), by **service** in step 3 (a select on each active row, `itcloud_service_contracts`, migration `0096`, `PUT /services/:id/contract`; wins over the customer's; the line is made or moved at once from the report kept at the last sync, `setServiceContract`; "Non associé" removes the line again), or **from the client's contract** (Contracts tab › services › **"Associer un service ITCloud (n)"**, `GET|POST /api/crm/clients/:id/contracts/:kid/itcloud-services`, `crm.billing`: the client's unmapped, active ITCloud services whose product is a portal service, ticked and put on that contract at once, `unmappedServices`; `itcloud_services.portal_service_id`, migration `0097`). ## Service prices and the contracts that use them

A price (or cost) is never moved on other contracts without asking (`server/lib/servicePrices.ts`, `src/components/ScopeDialog.tsx`; test "a price changed on a service, or on a contract line, asks how far it goes"). "Running contracts" = active ones.
- **Changing a service's price or cost** (Inventory › Services, `PATCH /api/services/:id`, `services.manage`): when it really changes and the service is on lines of running contracts, the server answers 409 `price_scope_required` (`fields: [{ path: "contracts", message: "<how many>" }]`) and the page asks: **yes, also in those contracts** (`applyToContracts: true`: every line of the service in a running contract gets the new price / cost, one "service changed" event on each, monthly figures re-worked) or **no, only the service, for future additions** (`false`; the contracts keep theirs). A change of anything else asks nothing.
- **Changing a price on a contract line** (the line editor of the contract window, `PATCH /clients/:id/contracts/:kid/services/:sid`, `crm.billing`; **ITCloud lines too — their price can be edited, their quantity and day stay the vendor's**, `service_from_vendor`): a new price asks first (409 `price_scope_required`, `message` = how many OTHER running contracts have the service) with three ways: **only this contract** (`scope: "line"`), **this contract and the service's price, for future additions** (`"service"`) or **this contract, the service and the other running contracts** (`"all"`, offered only when there are others). A quantity or day change asks nothing.

## Security rules

- Authorization is enforced in the API, never only in the UI. Use `auth(c)` / `requirePermission`; never trust ids or roles sent by the browser.
- Mutations: JSON only + same-origin check (`sameOriginJson`). Sessions are server-side (`sessions` table, cookie holds a random token; DB stores its SHA-256).
- Passwords: scrypt (`server/lib/crypto.ts`), policy ≥ 12 chars. MFA (TOTP) required in production (off by default in dev, `REQUIRE_MFA=true` to test); codes are single-use. MFA secrets sealed with `APP_SECRET`.
- **Signing in with a push notification on the phone** (migration `0091`; `server/lib/pushAuth.ts`, `server/routes/pushAuth.ts` at `/api/auth/push`; UI `src/lib/push.ts`, `PhoneApproval` in `LoginPage.tsx`, `src/pages/ApprovePage.tsx` at `/approve`, `src/components/PhoneSignInCard.tsx` in the profile; `public/sw.js` + `manifest.webmanifest`; packages `web-push`, `@simplewebauthn/server|browser`): a way to satisfy the second step instead of the authenticator code (which always stays as the fallback; it only applies where MFA is required, `REQUIRE_MFA`). **Enrolling** (a fully signed-in session, **the password asked again**, rate limited, not in "view as client"): the phone makes a **passkey** (WebAuthn, platform authenticator, `userVerification: required`: the private key stays in the phone's secure hardware and is unlocked by its fingerprint / face / PIN), subscribes to web push and gets a random **device token** (only its SHA-256 is kept; the push subscription is sealed with `APP_SECRET`); table `push_devices`; a bell alert `security.alert` tells the person. **Signing in**: after the password the session exists but is not allowed anything; `POST /start` makes a `push_challenges` row **bound to that session** (single use, 2 minutes, 6 an hour per person, 15 s apart) with a random nonce and a **number shown on the computer**; the push carries no secret (only the request id); the phone (`/approve`, no session) proves itself with its device token and shows where the request comes from (browser / OS, IP, time) and **three numbers** (number matching: approving by reflex fails; two wrong numbers close the request); the passkey signs the nonce (verified against the stored public key, origin and RP id, user verified, signature counter) and only then `markSessionMfaVerified` unlocks **that one session** — an approval never unlocks another browser. **"Ce n'est pas moi"** closes that session, rests the account 15 minutes and tells the administrators (`notifyUserLocked`) and the person. **Push endpoints are checked** (`pushEndpointOk`: https, no port or credentials, and only the browsers' push services — googleapis.com, push.services.mozilla.com, push.apple.com, notify.windows.com — so a subscription can never make the portal send a request to an address of the person's choosing); enrolling needs HTTPS in production. The waiting browser looks at `GET /auth/push/status` every second through one chain of timeouts (and at once when the tab comes back to the screen) — never in an effect that depends on a function recreated at each render (that stopped the check from ever running). **When a push cannot be sent the reason is kept** (`pushDetailed` returns the push service's status and a few harmless words: `refused` 401/403 (e.g. `BadJwtToken` = the VAPID keys of the server are not the ones the phone subscribed with), `gone` 404/410 (the phone is removed), `throttled` 429, `service` 5xx, `network` (no answer: the server cannot reach the push service over HTTPS), `bad_subscription`): it is in the server log (`[push] send to "…" failed`), in the audit event `push.challenge_sent` (`failures`), on the sign-in page under the error, and in the answer of the profile's Tester button. A sign-in is limited to 10 requests an hour per person, 15 s apart; the page names the limit when it is hit. Phones can be removed from the profile (or are removed when an administrator resets the person's MFA, or when the push service says the subscription is gone); everything is audited (`push.*`). Web push keys (VAPID) are made once and kept sealed in `integrations` provider `push`; the RP id / origin come from `PUBLIC_URL` (HTTPS in production; the server must reach the phones' push services over HTTPS); optional `PUSH_CONTACT` is the VAPID contact email. **iPhone / iPad**: notifications only work for the portal added to the home screen (the profile says so). Tests: `tests/api.test.ts` ("sign-in approved on the phone"), with a software authenticator (real WebAuthn registration and assertions) and web push mocked.
- **The portal's notifications on the phone** (migration `0092`; `server/lib/pushNotify.ts`): the phones enrolled for sign-in also get the bell's notifications as web push — `notify()` (`server/lib/notify.ts`) hands each batch to `pushOut` after saving it, never waiting and never failing. Per phone a switch `push_devices.notify` (on at enrolment; `PATCH /api/auth/push/devices/:id { notify }`, own phones only) and a test button (`POST /devices/:id/test`, 6 per 5 min); per person `user_preferences.push_details` (**off by default**: the lock screen only says the kind — "Nouveau texto", "Tâche pour vous" — and "Touchez pour ouvrir le portail"; on adds the ticket number / title / text, `pushText`), set in the profile (`PUT /api/me/preferences { pushDetails }`). The payload is encrypted end to end (RFC 8291, the push service only carries it), carries a **path of the portal** only (`safePath`: never another site; the service worker checks it again), at most 30 pushes per person per 10 minutes, and a phone the push service says is gone is removed. Tests: "the portal's notifications reach the enrolled phone".
- Admin changes are written to `audit_log` via `audit()`. Never log secrets.
- "View as client" (`clientview.use`): the session stores `view_as_client_id`; `loadAuth` swaps `auth.user` to a read-only client identity (`auth.realUser` is the technician). `readOnlyViewAs` blocks every write except `/api/view-as` and logout. Client endpoints must scope by `auth.user.clientId`.
- No secrets in `VITE_*` variables or committed files; `.env` only.
- CSP lives in `server/app.ts` (`secureHeaders`) — fonts are self-hosted (`@fontsource`), no external scripts.
- Graph paths built from browser input go through `safeGraphPath` (in `graph()`): no `.`/`..` segment once decoded, no `%2f`/`%5c`/whitespace. Organisation-access mailboxes (`via = "app"`) need `outlook.anyMailbox` on every request.
- `assertMasterGuard` also protects MASTER_ONLY rights: a non-master cannot give a role carrying one, nor edit/reset/disable someone holding one.
- Password links: built from `PUBLIC_URL` only in production (never the Host header); cleared on password, email or status change (`forgetPasswordLinks`). Client users of an inactive client cannot sign in (`accountUsable`).
- Nobody approves their own timesheet or expense. Invoice creation is one transaction. QuickBooks creations carry a `requestid` (idempotent) and run one at a time.
- One app instance only (in-process locks, numbering, background jobs).

## Production

Docker Compose on a Debian server; Nginx Proxy Manager on another server terminates HTTPS and forwards to port 3000. Owner runs deployments (see INSTALL.md). Never `docker compose down -v`.
